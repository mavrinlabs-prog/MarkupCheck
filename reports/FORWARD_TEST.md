# Forward test: the shipped model on loans it never saw

Generated by `src/forward_test.py`; do not edit by hand. Numbers are in APR percentage points.

**What we did.** We froze the shipped models (`app/model/`, both views, with the conformal widths in `meta.json`) and scored CarMax loans from three bond deals filed after training ended: CarMax Auto Owner Trust 2026-2 (52,812 loans), 2026-3 (79,198 loans), 2026-4 (69,556 loans). We parsed them with the project's CarMax parser, cleaned them with the same counted steps as training, and kept only loans originated after 2026-01, the newest month in the training data. Nothing was refitted or re-tuned.

**Loans tested:** 113,200, originated 2026-02 to 2026-08 (7 months). The 5-year Treasury yields for these months were already in `data/benchmark/treasury_5y_monthly.csv`.

| measure | forward test (CarMax, new months) | original test, CarMax only | original test, all lenders (README) |
|---|---|---|---|
| typical-price error, model | 1.72 | 1.55 | 2.53 |
| typical-price error, same-lender model | 1.62 | 1.46 | 2.35 |

| mean of actual minus typical price, model | -0.37 | -0.18 | |

The fair comparison is the middle column: the forward loans are all CarMax, and CarMax loans were already the easier ones in the original test months.

**What it shows.** The typical price held up: error 1.72 points against 1.55 on CarMax's original test loans, and the model beat the rule of thumb in 7 of 7 months. The 80% range did not hold up: it covered 68.8% of the new loans (65.7% for prime used-car buyers). 23.9% of loans fell below the range and 7.3% above it (9.8% and 7.1% on CarMax's original test loans). On average the new loans were priced 0.37 points below the model's typical price, so most misses are on the cheap side, not the side the markup flag uses. The rule of thumb's range lost about as much (81.4% to 67.0%), so CarMax's prices moved, not only the model. For a CarMax buyer this means an offer can look more ordinary than it is. Keeping the 80% promise would need the range recalibrated on new filings, which the shipped app does not do.

## Limits
- CarMax only: Ally and Santander have no public deal after 2026-1 (SEC filing index, Jul to Nov 2026), so this does not test the lenders where the model was weakest.
- CarMax is both the dealer and the lender on its own loans, so these loans say little about dealer markups.
- The B1 rule is rebuilt from the original training months; the conformal widths and flag thresholds come unchanged from `app/model/meta.json`.

## Capital One Prime Auto Receivables Trust 2026-1: a later deal from a lender in the data

A bank lending through dealers. Its 2024-1 and 2025-1 deals are in the training data, so this later deal tests a known lender's newer pool. Same parser and counted cleaning; the one change is a code map: Capital One labels every credit score "FICO", which we treat as the consumer bureau score that cleaning keeps. This section scores it with the whole-market model. The pool is prime only (scores 740 to 889) and its loans after cleaning were originated 2025-09 to 2026-01. The comparison column is the original test loans from the same months with scores of 740 or more.

| measure | Capital One 2026-1 | original test, same months and scores |
|---|---|---|
| loans | 44,690 | 27,039 |
| typical-price error, model | 1.19 | 1.18 |
| typical-price error, rule of thumb (B1) | 1.45 | 1.74 |
| 80% range covers, model | 77.1% | 89.3% |
| 80% range covers, rule of thumb (B1) | 69.3% | 85.3% |
| loans below / above the model's range | 22.3% / 0.6% | 4.4% / 6.3% |
| real loans flagged 'well above', model | 0.1% | 3.3% |
| mean of actual minus typical price | -0.83 | +0.09 |

**What it shows.** The model still beat the rule of thumb overall (1.19 vs 1.45 points), though not for super-prime new cars (1.01 vs 0.86), super-prime used cars (1.10 vs 0.97). But Capital One charged on average 0.83 points less than the model's typical price: 22.3% of its loans fell below the 80% range and 0.6% above it, and 23 of 44,690 were flagged 'well above'. For a buyer whose lender is a prime bank like this one, the whole-market range sits too high, so an offer marked up above that bank's usual price can still look typical.
